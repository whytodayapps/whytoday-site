Pulse — Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-10-30

Pulse — Decibel Meter ("Pulse") is an Android app by WhyToday. This policy says what Pulse does with information about you. Contact: whytodaysupport@gmail.com.

Short version

  • Sound is analysed on your phone. Audio and sound levels never leave it. Pulse has no account and no server of its own.
  • Three services receive some data: Sentry (crash reports), Google Analytics for Firebase (usage statistics) and, in the free version only, Google AdMob (ads). Crash reports and usage statistics are off unless you turn them on: Pulse asks once, the first time you open it, and each has its own switch in Settings.
  • Pulse never reads your contacts or files. It reads your location only if you tap "Add this phone's location" on a session, and that location stays on your phone.

What stays on your phone

  • Microphone. Pulse uses the microphone only while the meter is on screen or while a recording session you started is running. During a session Android shows a "Pulse is recording" notification for as long as the session lasts, including with the screen locked.
  • Sessions. Each recording is saved on the phone as a log of one sound level per second, with the notes you type: a title, a place, what made the noise, how it affected you, markers and a limit line. "Place" is text you type.
  • Location (optional). Only when you tap "Add this phone's location" in a session's details, Pulse asks Android for the phone's approximate position once, rounds it to about 1 km and saves it with that session. It is never read in the background, never sent to Pulse, Sentry, Google Analytics or AdMob, and it is printed on that session's report only. Remove it in the session's details, or refuse the location permission and Pulse never reads it.
  • Audio. Saved only if you turn on "Save audio with sessions" (off by default). The audio file stays on the phone and is deleted with its session.
  • Settings and calibration, and whether you bought Pro.

You delete a session in History. Uninstalling Pulse deletes everything it stored.

Reports. When you export a PDF or CSV report, Pulse hands the file to the Android share sheet. It goes only where you send it.

What leaves your phone

Crash reports: Sentry

Only if you turned on "Send Crash Reports": when Pulse crashes or hits an error, it sends a report to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.): the error type and message, the stack trace, the app version, the phone model and the Android version. Before sending, Pulse removes file paths and anything that looks like a sound level from error text. Reports contain no audio, no sound levels, no session data, no screenshots and no user identifier. Like any internet service, Sentry sees the network address a report comes from; Pulse does not add it to the report.

Sentry keeps error events for the retention period of the Sentry plan Pulse uses (Sentry states up to 90 days). Turn this on or off in Settings, "Send Crash Reports".

Usage statistics: Google Analytics for Firebase

Only if you turned on "Share Usage Statistics", Pulse records a small fixed list of events: the Pro page was opened (and from where), Pro was bought or restored, a session was saved (its length in broad bands such as "10 to 60 minutes", and whether audio was kept), the free session limit was reached, a report was exported (Pro, free with an ad, or free offline), sessions were compared (how many), calibration was saved. Google Analytics also collects what it collects in every app: a random app-instance id, the phone model, Android version, app version, language, country-level location derived from the network address, app opens, and in-app purchase events (the product, its name and price).

Events never contain audio, sound levels, session data, notes, file paths or location. Pulse turns off advertising-id collection for Analytics and always denies Analytics the use of data for ads.

Where the law requires consent (for example the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), Analytics stays off until you accept the consent form. Google keeps user-level Analytics data for the retention period set in the Pulse Analytics property (Google offers 2 or 14 months). Turn this on or off in Settings, "Share Usage Statistics".

Ads: Google AdMob (free version only)

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. AdMob receives the advertising id, the network address, phone and app information and how you interact with ads, to show ads, limit how often you see them, and prevent fraud. Where the law requires consent, a Google consent form asks you first, and you can change your choice later in Settings, "Privacy Choices". Without consent, ads are not personalised. How Google uses this information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

With Pro, Pulse shows no ads.

Purchases: Google Play

Pro is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment; Pulse only learns whether your Google account owns Pro. Pulse never sees payment details.

Deleting data held by these services

Pulse sends no name, email or account id, so neither we nor these services can match their data to you on request. Data held by Sentry and Google expires after the retention periods above. To stop sending it, turn off the switches in Settings, or reset your phone's advertising id in Android settings.

Children

Pulse is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If what Pulse collects changes, this policy changes in the same release, and the effective date above changes with it.
