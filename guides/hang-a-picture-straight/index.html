How to hang a picture straight with your phone

Your phone can do the job of a spirit level well enough to hang a picture straight. The trick is to level the nail marks before you put a hole in the wall, not the frame afterwards. This guide shows how, in about ten minutes.

You need

  • Your phone with a level app (AngleMate, or the level in your phone's built-in tools)
  • Painter's tape or masking tape
  • A pencil and a tape measure
  • Nails, picture hooks or wall anchors that suit the weight of the frame

Before you start: check that your phone reads true

A phone measures tilt with its motion sensor, and most phones are a little off. A case, a camera bump or a button can tilt it further. Thirty seconds of checking saves a second hole in the wall.

  1. Take the case off, or at least check that the edge you will hold against the frame is flat.
  2. Do the flip test. Hold the long edge of the phone against a door frame or a table edge and note the reading. Turn the phone end over end and hold the same edge against the same spot. If the two readings are the same distance from zero in opposite directions, the phone reads true and the surface is what is tilted. If they differ, your phone has an offset.
  3. Allow for any offset. Add the two readings and halve the result: that is how far your phone is off. For example, +0.6° and −0.2° means the phone reads 0.2° high, so treat 0.2° as level. An offset under half a degree will not show on most pictures.

Step by step

  1. Choose the height

    Galleries hang art so the centre of the picture sits at roughly eye level: about 145 to 152 cm (57 to 60 in) from the floor. Over a sofa or a sideboard, leave 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) between the furniture and the bottom of the frame instead. Mark the centre point lightly in pencil.

  2. Copy the hangers onto tape

    Lay the frame face down. Stretch a strip of tape across the back so it touches both hangers (D-rings, sawtooth hooks or the top of a pulled-tight wire). Mark the exact centre of each hanger on the tape. Two hangers hold a frame straight far better than one.

  3. Put the tape on the wall and level it

    Peel the tape off and stick it on the wall where the top of the frame should go. Hold the long edge of your phone along the tape and open the bubble level. Lift or lower one end of the tape until the phone reads level: 0.0°, or your phone's offset from the check above. This is the step that makes the picture straight.

  4. Fix the nails or hooks through the marks

    Drive the nails or screws straight through your pencil marks on the tape, then pull the tape away. Use a stud or a wall anchor for anything heavy, such as a mirror or a large canvas.

  5. Hang it and check the top edge

    Hang the picture, rest the phone on the top edge of the frame and read the angle. Nudge the frame until it reads level. If the top edge is too thin for the phone, hold the phone flat against the frame's side instead and use the vertical reading. In AngleMate you can lock the reading on screen, which helps when the phone is above your eye line.

  6. Step back and look

    Look at it from across the room. If a nearby ceiling, shelf or window frame is not level, a picture that is perfectly level can still look crooked beside it. In that case, line the picture up with what the eye sees, not with the level.

Hanging a row of frames or a gallery wall

For a row, a phone placed on one frame at a time is slow, and small errors add up from frame to frame. Two ways to do better:

  • One long strip of tape. Run a single strip across the whole row at the height of the hangers, level it with the phone at several points along its length, and mark every frame's hangers on that one strip.
  • The camera guide. AngleMate's Laser Level tool shows the rear camera with an alignment guide and an angle reading. Stand back, hold the phone square to the wall at the height of the frames, and line the guide up with the top edges to see whether they sit on one line. It does not project a laser onto the wall, so it is for checking what you have marked, not for drawing a line.

Hold the phone square to the wall when you use the camera. Seen from an angle, straight lines lean in the picture and the guide will mislead you.

If the picture will not stay straight

  • It tilts again after a day. A single nail or a wire lets the frame swing. Use two hooks, or add small felt or rubber bumpers to the bottom corners so the frame grips the wall.
  • Level, but it looks wrong. Old houses often have sloping ceilings and floors. Match the nearest strong line, such as a ceiling, mantel or door frame.
  • The reading jumps around. Hold the phone still for a second, or turn up smoothing in AngleMate's App Settings.

How accurate is a phone level?

Accurate enough for pictures, mirrors and small shelves once you have done the flip test. A phone's short edge and its sensor make it less reliable than a good spirit level over long distances, so for a long shelf, a countertop or anything that has to carry a load, check with a proper level. AngleMate is made for everyday alignment jobs like these, not for certified precision work.
