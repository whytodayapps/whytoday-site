How to level a washing machine with your phone

A washer that shakes, bangs or creeps across the floor during the spin cycle is usually not level, or it rocks on one foot. You can fix both with your phone and a wrench in about fifteen minutes.

You need

  • Your phone with a level app (AngleMate, or the level in your phone's built-in tools)
  • An adjustable wrench or pliers that fit the washer's feet
  • A helper is useful for front-loaders, which are heavy to tilt

Before you start

  • Empty the drum and put the machine where it will stay. Moving it afterwards undoes the work.
  • New front-loader? Check that the shipping bolts on the back have been removed. A washer that still has them will shake hard no matter how level it is. Your manual shows where they are.
  • Take the phone case off. A case or a camera bump stops the phone lying flat. Lie the phone screen-up so it rests on its flat back, with the camera bump hanging over the edge of the top panel if it would otherwise rock.
  • Check the phone reads true. Lay it on a table, note the reading, turn it end over end in the same spot and read again. If the two readings are the same distance from zero in opposite directions, the phone is accurate. If they are not, half their sum is your phone's offset; treat that number as level.

Step by step

  1. Find a flat spot on top

    Use the flat metal top panel, not a lid or a control panel that is moulded or sloped. On a top-loader, close the lid and use the flat cabinet edge beside it, or check each side separately.

  2. Read side to side and front to back

    Lay the phone flat in the middle of the top. A bubble level with a round, bull's-eye view such as AngleMate's shows both directions at once: one reading for side to side and one for front to back. Note which corner is low. If your level only reads one direction, turn the phone 90° and measure again.

  3. Adjust the front feet

    Most washers have screw-in feet at the front. Many have a locking nut on each foot that must be loosened first. Turn the foot out to raise that corner, in to lower it. Tilting the machine back a little takes the weight off the foot and makes it easier to turn.

    You will be down at floor level while the phone is on top. In AngleMate, turn on Sound: the phone sounds and vibrates when it reads level, so you can turn the feet without standing up to look.

  4. Set the back feet

    Some washers have screw-in feet at the back too; adjust them the same way. Many top-loaders have self-adjusting rear feet instead: tip the machine forward a few centimetres and set it down again so the back legs settle. Check your manual, because not every model works this way.

  5. Recheck and lock the feet

    Read the level again in both directions and repeat until both readings are as close to zero as you can get. Then tighten each locking nut up against the cabinet, so the feet cannot turn as the machine vibrates. Read the level once more afterwards; tightening can shift it slightly.

  6. Do the rock test, then a spin

    Press down firmly on opposite corners, one pair and then the other. If the machine rocks, one foot is not touching the floor: lower that foot until the rocking stops, then recheck the level. Finally run a short spin with a few towels in the drum and watch for shaking or movement.

Still shaking?

  • It rocks but reads level. Rocking does more harm than a fraction of a degree of tilt. Get all four feet firmly on the floor first, then fine-tune the level.
  • Small loads shake the most. A single heavy item, like a bath mat or a pair of jeans, can unbalance the drum. Add a few towels to spread the weight.
  • The floor flexes. On a wooden floor or upstairs, the floor itself can bounce. A solid board under the machine, or anti-vibration pads, can help.
  • It still bangs on a level, steady machine. Worn suspension springs or shock absorbers are a repair job. Contact the manufacturer or a technician.

Dryers, fridges and other appliances

A dryer, dishwasher or stove levels the same way: read both directions on a flat top or a shelf inside, then turn the feet. A fridge is different. Many are designed to lean back very slightly so the doors swing shut on their own, so check the manual before you set one perfectly level.

How accurate is a phone level?

Accurate enough to stop a washer walking, once you have done the flip test. The bigger risk is a phone that does not lie flat, so take the case off and use a flat part of the top. AngleMate is made for everyday alignment jobs like this, not for certified precision work.
